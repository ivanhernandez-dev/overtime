# 0024. Battery protection can be turned off, the helper's floor is configurable, and the battery temperature has limits

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Accepted:** 2026-10-17. The project author decided that the app's battery protection can be switched off, that the helper keeps a battery floor that is configurable but can never be removed, and that Overtime gets a battery temperature protection with configurable degrees. The ranges, the hysteresis and the tie-breaks below were delegated to Claude; the author may revisit them.
- **Date:** 2026-10-17
- **Deciders:** Project author
- **Related requirements:** **BAT-01**, **BAT-04**, **BAT-05**, **HOLD-16**, **HOLD-17**, **HOLD-18**, **APP-13**
- **Related ADRs:** [0009](0009-helper-release-sequence.md), [0010](0010-pinned-helper-binary.md), [0011](0011-helper-battery-floor.md), [0012](0012-helper-thermal-release.md), [0019](0019-detection-loop-energy-design.md)

## Context

The author asked for battery protection to be optional and for its degrees to be configurable: the battery percentages, including the helper's fixed 10%, and a temperature protection with the degrees at which it acts. The safety choice was "optional with a minimum floor": the switch turns off the app's own battery threshold, but the opt-in root helper keeps a minimum that can never be removed. A changed helper binary, and the one-time re-approval it brings, was accepted.

- **The helper's floor (ADR [0011](0011-helper-battery-floor.md)).** While the helper holds `SleepDisabled`, macOS cannot emergency-sleep the Mac, so the helper releases on its own at 10%. Since the H-CR-01 change it reads the charge whatever the power source, because a charger that supplies less than the Mac draws drains the battery while macOS reports external power. It also releases at macOS's early low-battery warning and when the power state can't be read.
- **The thermal release (ADR [0012](0012-helper-thermal-release.md)).** The same gate blocks thermal-emergency sleep, so the helper releases at the Trapping thermal pressure level, at `.critical`, and when the thermal state can't be read.
- **Where the battery temperature lives.** A read-only probe on macOS 27 found no top-level `Temperature` on the `AppleSmartBattery` IORegistry entry. The value is in its child entry `AppleSmartBatteryPack`, inside the `BatteryData` dictionary, as an integer in centi-degrees Celsius (3309 is 33.09 degrees). `IORegistryEntrySearchCFProperty` finds the top-level `BatteryData` first, which has no temperature, so it is not used. One read took about 32 microseconds on the author's Mac, unprivileged and without spawning a process. A Mac without a battery has no reading. CPU and SoC temperatures need private SMC or HID APIs.

## Decision

- **The battery protection switch (BAT-04).** `batteryProtectionEnabled`, on by default. Off, a known charge never makes the app release the Mac, and switching it off ends an active battery release with `BATTERY unblock reason=off`. An unknown charge on battery still releases, as with the switch on: it is a failure state, and when the state is unclear, never stuck awake wins. The switch removes a comfort limit, not the failure handling. The helper's floor applies either way.
- **The helper's floor (HOLD-18).** `helperBatteryFloorPercent`, from 5% to 50%, default 10%, on any power source (the H-CR-01 rule), with battery protection on or off. The lower bound is 5%, not 3%: on battery, macOS's early low-battery warning at 10% already releases the helper, so a floor under 10% only acts on external power that can't keep up. There the drain continues while the release runs and the Mac enters sleep, the helper reads whole percents rounded down, and powerd's final warning is at 2% with the SMC cut near 1%. 5% keeps three points before the final warning; 3% would keep one. The helper never trusts the client's number: a requested value of 0 or less means "not given" and gives the compiled default, and any other value is clamped into the compiled range (`HelperSafetyLimits`). The app stops asking for the helper at the same floor, so it is not refused.
- **Temperature protection (BAT-05).** `temperatureProtectionEnabled`, on by default, and `temperatureReleaseCelsius`, from 35 to 60 degrees Celsius, default 45. At or above the release temperature the app releases the Mac exactly like the battery floor (`TEMPERATURE block`, `WANT off reason=temperature`, the `temperatureReleases` counter, one Battery temperature notification per hot spell with the lid open, one summary line with the lid closed). It holds again strictly below the release temperature minus 5 degrees. The resume threshold is a fixed hysteresis, not a second setting: battery temperature moves slowly, a second value would add a coupled validation rule and a second row, and the helper needs no resume value at all. The app tells three readings apart: a temperature, unavailable (no battery entry, or no value in it or its packs) and unreadable (a value that is not a whole number, outside -40 to 100 degrees, or a read that timed out). Unavailable never blocks and ends a block; the Battery pane says Unavailable. Unreadable keeps the last state: blocked stays blocked, holding stays holding.
- **The helper's temperature ceiling (HOLD-18).** The helper also releases at or above a battery temperature ceiling: the release temperature while temperature protection is on, otherwise 60 degrees, clamped to 35 to 60, with 60 for a missing value, on any power source, through the unchanged release sequence of ADR [0009](0009-helper-release-sequence.md). A missing or unreadable battery temperature sets no ceiling for that check. This does not weaken "never stuck awake": the ceiling is an extra limit on top of the protections that already guarantee it. On the rootless path macOS's own thermal emergency sleep still works, and on the helper path the mandatory thermal-pressure release of ADR 0012 (which also releases on an unreadable thermal state) stays the helper's thermal fail-safe; failing closed on a missing battery value would make the helper unusable on a Mac whose battery entry differs. The app mirrors the ceiling with the same 5-degree hysteresis, so a reading that wavers around 60 degrees with temperature protection off cannot make it engage and release the helper on every poll.
- **The XPC shape and helper version 2.** The set method becomes `setSleepDisabled(_:batteryFloorPercent:temperatureCeilingCelsius:reply:)` and replaces the two-argument one: two integers, not a payload, so no parser runs as root, the interface stays two methods whose ObjC type encodings a test pins, and "absent" stays expressible as 0. A clear ignores both integers. An old app talking to the new helper, or the reverse, gets an XPC error and sets nothing, the safe outcome, and the version handshake then retires the stale helper (ADR [0010](0010-pinned-helper-binary.md)). The helper binary changes, so `HelperIdentity.version` is 2 and a release needs the signed pin `Pinned/helper/v2`.
- **The limits are re-sent when they change.** The app records the limits it sent with an engage. When the settings change them while the helper is engaged, the next event sends a new engage with the new limits (`HOLD helper engage reason=limits`). A repeated set from the same connection refreshes the helper's session and keeps its ownership; a set whose new limits are unsafe is refused and releases. Every accepted set logs the effective floor and ceiling.
- **The cadence (APP-13).** The app reads the battery temperature in `PowerSourceMonitor`, on the same 30 s timer and power-source notifications that already drive the power reading, and publishes it on a second stream: no new timer, no process. It publishes whole degrees, rounded down, and only when the value changes, so the engine snapshot changes at most once per degree. Rounding down loses nothing: with whole-degree thresholds, `reading >= release` and `reading < release - 5` give the same answers on the floored value as on the exact one. The helper reads the temperature only in its existing 5 s watch, its set and its status.

## Consequences

- **The helper is approved again once.** Every unplug protection user approves the helper once more after the update that ships helper version 2: Settings > Unplug Protection shows Needs your approval again, and the user turns Overtime off and on under Allow in the Background. README and CHANGELOG say so.
- **Floors under 10% act only on external power**, because on battery macOS's early warning releases the helper at 10% anyway. The Battery pane caption says that unplug protection also stops at macOS's low-battery warning.
- **Battery temperature only.** CPU and SoC temperatures need private SMC or HID APIs and stay out of scope; the helper's thermal-pressure release covers the Mac as a whole.
- **The panel reuses the battery limit state.** A hot battery shows the battery limit state with its own title ("Battery hot"), reason and timing line, and keeps the battery-low glyph: a new state would need a new glyph, and that glyph already means "keep-awake is off to protect the battery".
- **Logs stay ASCII.** The decision log says "degrees Celsius"; only the UI copy uses the degree sign.

## Alternatives considered

- **A configurable resume temperature.** A second setting with a validation rule tied to the release temperature, and a second row, for a value that a fixed 5 degrees covers.
- **A JSON payload over XPC.** A parser in the root process and a looser interface than two integers.
- **A separate timer for the temperature.** A second wake-up source for a value that the 30 s battery poll already reaches.
- **Releasing the helper on an unreadable battery temperature.** Fail-closed on a value some Mac models may not report would make the helper unusable there; the thermal-pressure release already fails closed.
- **A 3% floor.** One point above powerd's final warning while the battery may still be draining on a weak charger.
- **A new panel state with its own glyph.** A new asset for a state the battery-low glyph already describes.

## Not verified

- No battery was heated or drained on purpose: by rule, nothing here was exercised on hardware at a real limit.
- The pack layout was read on macOS 27 only. The top-level `Temperature` fallback covers older layouts by design and is untested on hardware.
- How the Battery pane looks and reads, with VoiceOver too, is a human check.
